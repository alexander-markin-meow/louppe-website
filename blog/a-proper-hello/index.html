Louppe becoming a real thing: introducing 1.9

Louppe 1.9 brings clearer controls and a simpler start to free photo, video and audio culling on Mac

Louppe started as a pet project, and now people are downloading it. For 1.9, I've focused on making it easier to find your way around from the first session

a place to make your choices

Louppe is a free media culler for Mac. You open a folder, go through what's in it, decide what you want to keep, and take those files into whatever comes next

You can review one item at a time or look through a grid, mark yes or no, add stars and colour labels, and export your choices. You don't need to import everything into a catalogue first. Your media stays on your Mac, and exporting makes copies by default

I'm an artist, and photos are a big part of how I use it. But I don't want Louppe to belong only to photography. It already works with video and audio too, and I want people working across different kinds of media to feel at home in it

The idea is pretty simple: a quiet place between having a folder full of things and knowing which ones you want to work with

making the first few minutes easier

A lot of the work in 1.9 is about helping the app explain itself

There's a proper Help window, searchable keyboard shortcuts, and review tips you can dismiss once you don't need them. I've also reorganised the command palette so it's easier to find an action without already knowing its exact name

Filters are clearer too. It should be easier to see what's active, understand why you're looking at a smaller set of files, and get back to everything. The export choices are more explicit, especially which items you've selected and what you're about to copy or move

Some of the changes are small: a more compact start window, a folder you can drop straight into it, quieter save feedback. Others make reviewing feel more natural, like a zoom slider and pinch-to-zoom for photos

I don't expect one update to make everything obvious. But I want fewer moments where you have to stop and work out what the app is asking you to do

a small project, with a bit more attention

Seeing people download Louppe makes me want to give it more care. It's still an independent project, and I'm still figuring out what it should become. But it isn't only something sitting on my own Mac anymore

That doesn't mean I want to keep adding things just to make the feature list longer. I'd like it to become easier to understand, more comfortable to use, and useful to people whose way of working is different from mine

Louppe is free and open source. There's no account to create, and the app has no telemetry. So if something feels confusing, a message from you is much more useful than me guessing how your first session went

give it a go, and tell me where you get stuck

Louppe 1.9 is available now. Give it a try, and I'd love to hear how it feels when you open it for the first time

What wasn't obvious? What did you expect to happen? What would make you want to use it again?

Send me a note — it comes straight to me

And if you're curious about the person behind the app, you'll find my other work at alex-markin.com
